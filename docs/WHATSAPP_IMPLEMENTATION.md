## Implemented

- Registered the connection repository and per-device runtime in Nest, replacing shared gateway registration. Webhook processing carries receiving-device context and rejects unknown devices, groups, self messages, and unauthorized senders.
- Added connection-scoped persistence, partial unique active-account indexes, atomic owner challenges, generation checks, scoped inbound deduplication, protected QR bytes, and database row locks that serialize side effects across processes without blocking unrelated accounts.
- Added authenticated QR login/asset, status, verification, owner replacement, reconnect, and disconnect endpoints. Local revocation immediately removes authority; provider cleanup retains bounded retry state. Account deletion checks provider cleanup.
- Replaced the shared notification sender with a lightweight dispatcher and durable per-connection/generation Redis queues. Redis global concurrency enforces one active delivery per queue across processes; queue discovery survives publisher restarts, and idle workers close after sixty seconds. Durable registry entries remain for discovery; each poll scans a bounded batch. Redis outages are logged while queued work remains pending.
- Bound new WhatsApp notification deliveries to their original connection generation. Existing settings record explicit proactive consent internally. Delivery rechecks preferences, retires unbound legacy work, records uncertain send outcomes, and generates a bounded deterministic missed-reminder summary after an outage.
- Replaced the WhatsApp domain service's rate, engagement, artificial-delay, quiet-hour, and reserved notification-command gates. Existing conversation and media boundaries remain in use.
- Rebuilt the WhatsApp section inline in Layanan: provider QR, separate owner verification with embedded copy control, account-specific edit controls, muted recovery actions, and confirmation dialogs. Telegram and Calendar remain visible. Mobile tabs fit the viewport.
- Disabled GoWA automatic media download; authorized inbound media uses on-demand download. The authenticated QR proxy validates provider origin/path, redirects, streamed size, PNG signature, and QR duration.
- Production/startup configuration now rejects a missing or empty webhook secret outside development/test. It reuses the existing configuration key.
- Applied three additive migrations to the local development database, including legacy queue retirement and delivery bindings. Historical product records remain intact.

### Recovery snapshot and retry correctness

Recovery now atomically claims the summary text and its exact covered delivery IDs under the connection lock. A safe pre-dispatch retry rebuilds both together, so late records cannot silently enlarge the coverage of old text. An already delivered or uncertain summary retains its stored coverage. Source validity is rechecked after acquiring the send lock; canceled reminders are removed from a safe retry, and an empty retry becomes obsolete.

The claim also stores a durable attempt counter. Five failed pre-dispatch attempts exhaust the budget and retain the missed records and a connection reason for inspection. A claim left attempting for sixty seconds becomes `unknown`, with a warning, rather than being sent again. Provider uncertainty is terminal for automatic replay. Unversioned prepared summaries upgrade their budget from recorded failures with a warning; missing scoped coverage for a terminal summary fails visibly instead of inventing evidence.

Recovery preparation/database errors no longer classify a provider-confirmed healthy connection as a new transport outage. They are logged as degraded recovery while the existing outage remains pending. Regressions were observed failing for stale retry content, cancellation after preparation, false transport disconnection, and recalculated coverage marking a later reminder as summarized. The suite also checks stale-claim uncertainty, ambiguous acknowledgment suppression, and retry exhaustion. The latest broad backend run, including historical-calendar validation, passes 200 tests across 16 suites; TypeScript and targeted lint also pass. These use real PostgreSQL and the domain service, with provider I/O stubbed. No new configuration or migration is required; the snapshot and counter use the existing binding payload.

The past-calendar recovery regression reproduced a valid missed appointment being silently dropped after its start time. Recovery now uses `CalendarReminderService.resolveMissed` through a scoped notification-service method, including the source check inside the send lock. It reuses normal calendar expansion for recurring occurrences. The ordinary resolver retains its after-start suppression. Real PostgreSQL/domain tests cover a valid past appointment, cancellation, a moved event, and a foreign-user payload; focused calendar tests cover recurrence identity, deletion, changed lead time, future due time, and malformed dates.

Pending notification deliveries now enter recovery collection before summary creation and before a delivery worker can send during the provider-confirmed/recovery-incomplete interval. Collection locks the connection and atomically transitions only pending/failed intents due between disconnection and the first confirmed recovery time. Reminder due times come from their occurrence relation; calendar intents persist their scheduling envelope in the existing binding payload at enqueue, and rearming refreshes that envelope while retaining the original generation. Delivered, attempting, and uncertain outcomes are not collected. Later provider polls preserve the first recovery boundary rather than extending the outage. Oversized episodes above 10,000 candidate records fail visibly and remain pending rather than silently producing partial coverage.

The delayed-worker regression failed before the fix: recovery cleared the outage and the delayed worker sent the ordinary reminder body. The updated path produces one labeled missed-reminder summary and preserves the original occurrence's missed evidence. Additional checks cover the worker waking before summary processing, delayed calendar collection, cancellation, rearming, pre-outage due times, and terminal/in-flight outcomes. A deliberate restoration of the old polling timestamp behavior made the post-recovery exclusion test fail. The final isolated broad run passes 200 tests across 16 suites; backend TypeScript and targeted lint pass. Backend processes resumed after the run, and GoWA remained running throughout.

The scheduling-stage audit remains open: occurrences whose notification intents have not yet been persisted by the upstream scheduler at recovery need durable outage/schedule evidence. This collection proves the delivery-worker boundary only; it does not prove every scheduling-stage outage case or historical notification-preference interval.
